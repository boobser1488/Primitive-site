Getting started

You wake with nothing. This page takes you from the menu to a fire, a knife and a full stomach.

Making a world

SINGLEPLAYER → NEW. The form asks for:

  • NAME and SEED. The same seed gives the same world to anyone who types it. Leave it empty for a random one; ROLL fills in a random number you can see.
  • WORLD TYPE: NORMAL is the game. TEST is a flat field with every station, crop and animal already built around the spawn, for trying things out.
  • GRASS DENSITY: how thick the meadow grows. It also decides how many grass tufts you can pull for fibre.
  • HOW BIG THE WORLD IS: a planet from THE EARTH 1:1 (the default, about 108 days to walk round) down to SMALL (about four hours). On a small planet every climate is within a day's walk.
  • THE GROUND MOVES: how often landslides and earthquakes happen.
  • COMMANDS AND TESTING: off by default. On, you are the operator in your own world: /time, /give, /tp and the G give menu work.

Picking where to land

A new world is a planet, and you choose where on it you wake. The globe can be turned by dragging, zoomed with the wheel (or a pinch), and you click land to put a cross on it. The panel on the right says what is there: the continent, the country, how warm and wet it is, how far the equator is, and what lies within a day's walk.

  • LAND HERE starts you at the cross (on the nearest flat, dry ground).
  • ANYWHERE lets the game pick a spot on a coast.

This is the one choice you cannot undo on foot. The tropics are hot, wet and have almost no winter. The dry belt is desert and savanna, where water is the problem. The temperate belt (oak, birch, meadows, a real winter) is what the game was built around. The north means spruce, frozen lakes and needing wool.

The first three things

Until you have done them, one line above the belt tells you the next step. There are three, in any order, and the line goes away for good once all are done:

  1. Pick up a stone. Pebbles lie loose on the ground. Break one (left mouse button), look at the stone that drops and press E to put it in your pack.
  2. Pull tall grass for fibre. Left-click a tuft of tall grass. Not every tuft gives fibre; reeds by the water give two each, so fibre is quicker at a river.
  3. Find flint and knap flakes from it.

After that, open the pack (I) and look at its LADDER tab. It shows the seven ages from bare hands to iron, where you stand, what the next one needs and what kind of place it is found in. See The path.

Where flint is

Flint is dark, angular nodules. Look for:

  • The pale strip along rivers and lakes. Gravel and pebbles wash up in a band a few blocks wide at the water's edge, and roughly one pebble in three there is flint. The sea has no such strip.
  • Bare rock and scree under cliffs, and cave floors, where a lot of it has fallen out of the rock.
  • Gravel: four gravel sift into one flint by hand.

The first tools

No tool is made in one click. Each comes from a head, a handle and a binding:

Each tool does its own job and nothing else: the pickaxe breaks stone and ore, the axe fells standing trees, the knife cuts grass, leaves and carcasses. Any of them helps with soft work like digging earth.

A freshly bound axe does not fell trees. The head wobbles in its binding: the stone axe handles deadfall and boards, and the stone pickaxe earth and gravel, but neither can take a standing trunk or stone. Wedge it first: the tool + 2 pegs, by hand. Pegs come from a worked stick (1 → 4).

Until you have a wedged axe, wood comes from fallen trunks and sticks lying on the ground.

Fire

The first fire needs no stones and no recipe. Drop three sticks and one log on the same spot (Q drops what is in your hand), then right-click the ground under them with flint in your hand. That is a firepit. If something is missing, the game says how many sticks and logs are lying there.

Later you can make a proper campfire: 3 cobblestone + 4 sticks. It can be picked up and carried; a firepit stays where it was lit. Cobblestone needs a (wedged) pickaxe.

  • Lighting a fire uses up one flint each time it actually catches.
  • Right-click a fire with anything other than flint to open its screen: four input slots, a fuel slot, two output slots and an ash slot. It works by itself: load it, light it, walk away.
  • A burning fire smokes, and the smoke column is visible from far off. It is the easiest way to find your camp again.
  • Standing in a fire burns you, four health a second. Step out.

Water

Drink by right-clicking water with an empty hand. Not all water is the same:

  • Running water (rivers, streams, a ditch dug from a river) is safe.
  • Standing water (ponds, lakes, puddles) quenches thirst but makes you ill two times in five: about a minute and a half of slowly losing health, with wounds not healing. The illness starts about two minutes after drinking.
  • Sea water makes you thirstier, and its illness lasts twice as long.

A jug of pond water placed in a burning hearth for twenty seconds becomes safe. Sea water cannot be boiled clean.

Food

Eat with F (the item in your selected slot). Berries and roots keep you going until you have a fire; meat and fields are what actually feed you. Cooked meat fills four times as much as raw, and raw meat makes you ill for 45 seconds. Meat comes off a carcass only with a knife: right-click the carcass with a knife, one cut per click.

The food bar holds 20. Standing still, you would barely get hungry; digging, running and jumping are what empty it. More in Survival.

The first night

Nights are dark and cold, coldest just before dawn. A torch is a stick and fibre; it comes out unlit, and you light it by right-clicking a burning fire with it in your hand. A grass torch lasts two and a half minutes. Any opaque block over your head halves the night's cold; a roof plus walls on three sides takes away four-fifths of it, and a fire inside the walls does the rest.
